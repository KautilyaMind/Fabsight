# Synthetic educational process-monitoring guide

> This is a synthetic educational document created for the FabSight simulation. It
> is not a real production SOP.

Process monitoring compares manufacturing observations with expected behavior. Basic
statistical process control concepts include a stable baseline, natural variation,
special-cause signals, control charts, and careful review of data quality. A control
signal is evidence for investigation; it is not by itself proof of a defect cause.

For deposition, lithography, etch, implantation, cleaning, and CMP, useful educational
questions include whether a change is persistent, whether multiple measurements move
together, whether the event timing aligns with maintenance or alarms, and whether an
inspection result repeats across lots. Anonymous variables should retain anonymous
names unless authoritative source metadata defines their physical meanings.

Equipment maintenance context can help organize an investigation. Preventive work,
calibration checks, inspections, and generic alarms should be reviewed chronologically.
Correlation between an alarm and a quality observation does not establish causality.
A structured investigation records competing explanations, conflicting evidence,
missing information, and the provenance of every reference.

Anomaly investigation begins with verification: confirm the sample identity, inspect
missing values, compare against a suitable baseline, and check whether the observation
is reproducible. Quality engineering favors traceable evidence over unsupported
conclusions.
